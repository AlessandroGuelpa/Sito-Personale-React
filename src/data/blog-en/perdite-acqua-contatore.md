If your water meter moves while everything it supplies is idle, it’s worth looking for a leak. The simplest test takes two readings two hours apart, with no water use in between. The difference tells you how much water passed through. A second test can help figure out where it went.

## How to take the two readings

Find the meter that serves your home. If you live in an apartment building and read the main meter, a neighbour’s water use will be included. You need a dedicated meter for this test. Next, check how it displays fractions of a cubic metre. One thousandth of a cubic metre is one litre. Use every digit you can read, even those your water utility might ask you to leave out when submitting a billing reading.

Turn off the taps, wait for the toilet cisterns to finish filling and choose a window when the washing machine and dishwasher will be idle. Include anything that can start on its own, such as irrigation or a water softener’s regeneration cycle. Photograph the meter at the start, note the time and take another reading two hours later. Leave the plumbing’s normal water supply open during the test so any leak downstream of the meter can still draw water.

## From three litres in two hours to a monthly volume

Suppose the meter shows 128.436 m³ at 2 p.m. and 128.439 m³ at 4 p.m. The difference is 0.003 m³, or 3 litres. Divide by the two-hour test window to get an average flow of 1.5 litres per hour. If that rate stayed constant, it would add up to 36 litres per day and 1,080 litres over 30 days, or 1.08 m³.

The test window is the only period you measured. The monthly figure assumes the flow continues at the same rate. For example, a cistern that refills for a few seconds at intervals can produce the same total as a small, steady flow. Two readings tell you how much water passed during the test. A third reading halfway through can help narrow down when it happened.

## How to check a suspected toilet leak

If you can access the water in the cistern, wait for it to fill and add a few drops of food colouring. Wait about ten minutes without flushing. If colour appears in the bowl, water is passing from the cistern. Flush immediately after the test to avoid staining. If you can identify and safely operate the toilet’s dedicated shut-off valve, repeat the meter test with the toilet supply closed. With everything else kept the same, a lower reading difference indicates that this branch is contributing to the problem. After a repair, repeat the test with the toilet supply open again.

If the meter keeps advancing across several tests with no known water use, keep the photos, times and volume differences. These will help whoever needs to locate the leak. If the meter stays still, consider the instrument’s sensitivity and the length of the test. An intermittent leak may stay inactive for the entire window. A display that shows only whole cubic metres can also miss a few litres if the reading never reaches the next increment. Check the model’s instructions to see whether it has a more sensitive flow indicator.

As with [measuring food waste before taking action](/blog/spreco-alimentare-misurare-prima), start with a baseline you can reproduce. Use the same duration and similar conditions before and after the repair. If the signal disappears, you have a practical way to verify the result within the limits of your meter.
