You want to see the step between the gate and your front door. You fit a brighter floodlight, but from the gate you see the bright lamp before you can make out the edge of the step. The hedge lights up too. Before buying a fixture with more lumens, check where the light from your current one goes.

## Lumens at the fixture, lux on the path

**Lumens** measure luminous flux. If the rating is for the complete fixture, it tells you how much light comes out in total. **Lux** measure illuminance on a surface. One lumen spread over one square metre gives one lux. The beam shape, mounting height and aiming angle affect how much light lands on the step.

Shielding the fixture above and aiming the beam at the path can help limit spill. Consider what happens after light hits the ground, too. Pale paving can reflect some of it upwards, so check the light level as well as where the beam points.

## Two 800-lumen fixtures, two results

Imagine two fixtures that each emit 800 lumens and a 10 m² stretch of path. With the wide beam, suppose 200 lumens reach the path directly. With the more focused beam, suppose 400 lumens reach it.

- **Wide beam.** 200 lm ÷ 10 m² = **20 lux on average** from direct light.
- **Focused beam.** 400 lm ÷ 10 m² = **40 lux on average** from direct light.

The numbers are hypothetical and show how the calculation works. If the second fixture can halve its output while keeping the same distribution, it will deliver 20 lux on average again. Before dimming it, check the least illuminated spots as well. A narrow beam can give a high average while leaving the step in shadow.

## How long does the light spill beyond the path?

In the Bernese Prealps, an experiment found **62% fewer nocturnal visits to flowers** in meadows lit with artificial lamps than in meadows left dark. The figure applies to the conditions of that study. To see why the area beside a path matters as well, consider [how habitats support networks of pollinators](/blog/biodiversita-impollinatori-reti).

A timer or a motion sensor can shorten the hours of use where continuous lighting is unnecessary. An 8 W lamp running for ten hours uses 80 Wh. If it runs for two hours in total, it uses 16 Wh, excluding any power the controller draws while the lamp is off. This follows from the [difference between power and energy](/blog/kw-kwh-potenza-energia).

## A check to make after dark

Walk the path in both directions. Check whether you can see the edge of the step, whether the fixture dazzles you at the entrance and how much light reaches the hedge beside the path. If you can adjust the angle, start by aiming the beam at the area you need to see. Then adjust the output and run time and walk the route again. With a motion sensor, make sure the light turns on **before** you reach the step and stays on long enough for you to reach the door.
