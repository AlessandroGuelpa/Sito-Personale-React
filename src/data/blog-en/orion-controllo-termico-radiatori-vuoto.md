A fan cools a computer by moving air over its heat sinks. A radiator on the outside of a spacecraft has to release heat without that airflow. Meanwhile, electronics and other systems continue to generate heat throughout the journey. Orion uses several forms of heat transfer inside the vehicle, then radiates energy away from its external surfaces.

The steady heat produced on a flight differs from the short, intense heating absorbed by [Orion's heat shield during re-entry](/blog/orion-rientro-scudo-termico). Both sit alongside other mission constraints, including [lunar launch windows](/blog/finestre-lancio-luna-orbite).

## Follow the heat from equipment to space

Air can circulate in a pressurised cabin. In the vacuum outside, the vehicle rejects heat by emitting electromagnetic radiation. Orion's European Service Module collects heat with cold plates and heat exchangers. A circulating fluid carries it to six radiator panels through two independent loops that operate together, as described by ESA. The fluid stays in the loops while the panels radiate.

The pump moves heat between parts of the vehicle. The amount the panels can release still depends on their radiating area, temperatures and surroundings. Increasing flow through the loop alone may leave the external heat rejection capacity unchanged.

## Calculate a simplified heat balance

Suppose equipment releases 600 W of heat and the radiator rejects 450 W under the conditions being considered. Set sunlight and other exchanges aside for this example. The remaining 150 W adds energy to the system. Over 600 seconds, that adds 90,000 joules, or 90 kJ. To estimate the temperature increase, you would also need the thermal capacity and the distribution of heat. These are illustrative figures.

## Check both hot and cold conditions

The radiators can also absorb incoming energy. The vehicle's orientation, exposure to sunlight and surface properties therefore matter. In cold conditions, the goal can shift to retaining heat or adding it. Orion uses multilayer insulation and electric heaters as part of its thermal design.

When reviewing a spacecraft's power budget, I would also trace where each active device sends its heat. Then I would check both the highest expected heat input and the cold case. That helps determine the conditions and duration in which the equipment can operate.
